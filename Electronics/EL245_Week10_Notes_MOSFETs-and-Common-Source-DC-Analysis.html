MOSFETs and common-source DC analysis

How a MOS gate creates a conducting channel, the current equations for n- and p-channel, enhancement and depletion devices, and how to find and design the DC operating point of a common-source circuit.

01The MOS structure

Reading Neamen 3.1.1 and 3.1.2; Lecture 21

The transistor family

Transistors split into bipolar junction transistors (npn, pnp) and field-effect transistors (FETs). FETs are junction FETs or metal-oxide-semiconductor FETs (MOSFETs). MOSFETs come in two modes, enhancement and depletion, each with an n-channel and a p-channel version. Unlike a BJT, a MOSFET's gate draws no DC current: the gate is insulated from the channel by an oxide.

The MOS capacitor

The basic MOS structure is a capacitor: a metal gate (for example aluminium), an insulating oxide of thickness \(t_{ox}\) and permittivity \(\varepsilon_{ox}\), and a semiconductor substrate as the second plate. For a p-type substrate:

Gate voltageWhat happens at the oxide-semiconductor interface
NegativeAccumulation: holes are attracted to the interface.
Small positiveDepletion: holes are pushed away, leaving a negative space-charge region of fixed acceptor atoms.
Larger positiveInversion: minority electrons are drawn to the interface and form an electron inversion layer.

An n-type substrate behaves the same way with all signs reversed: a positive gate accumulates electrons, and a large negative gate forms a hole inversion layer. The inversion charge grows with the applied voltage.

Definition

Enhancement mode: no channel exists at zero gate voltage; a gate voltage must be applied to create the inversion layer (positive for a p-type substrate, negative for an n-type substrate).

The n-channel enhancement MOSFET

Add two heavily doped n-type regions to the MOS capacitor, the source and the drain, and you have an n-channel enhancement MOSFET. The channel length \(L\) is typically below 1 µm and the oxide thinner than 400 Å.

Figure 1.1: With no gate voltage, the source and drain are separated by p-type material: two back-to-back diodes, so essentially no current flows. A large enough positive gate voltage forms the electron inversion layer (dashed), which connects source and drain. The source supplies the carriers and the drain collects them.

The drain current depends on the charge in the inversion layer, which depends on the gate voltage. No current flows through the substrate or through the gate.

02NMOS current-voltage characteristics

Reading Neamen 3.1.3; Lecture 22

The threshold voltage \(V_{TN}\) is the gate-source voltage needed to create the inversion layer. Below it the transistor is off (\(i_D = 0\)). Above it, a small \(v_{DS}\) gives a current proportional to \(v_{DS}\). As \(v_{DS}\) rises, the channel near the drain thins, until at \(v_{DS(sat)} = v_{GS} - V_{TN}\) it is pinched off. Beyond that the current stops increasing: the transistor is in the saturation region.

NMOS drain current\[ \text{non-saturation } (v_{DS} \lt v_{DS(sat)}):\quad i_D = K_n\left[2(v_{GS} - V_{TN})v_{DS} - v_{DS}^2\right] \] \[ \text{saturation } (v_{DS} \ge v_{DS(sat)}):\quad i_D = K_n(v_{GS} - V_{TN})^2 \qquad v_{DS(sat)} = v_{GS} - V_{TN} \]
\(v_{DS}\)
Drain-source voltage (V).
\(v_{DS(sat)}\)
Drain-source voltage at the boundary between the two regions (V).
\(i_D\)
Drain current (A).
\(K_n\)
Conduction parameter (A/V²).
\(v_{GS}\)
Gate-source voltage (V).
\(V_{TN}\)
Threshold voltage; positive for an enhancement NMOS (V).
Conduction parameter\[ K_n = \frac{W\mu_n C_{ox}}{2L} = \frac{k_n'}{2}\cdot\frac{W}{L} \qquad k_n' = \mu_n C_{ox} \qquad C_{ox} = \frac{\varepsilon_{ox}}{t_{ox}} \]
\(K_n\)
Conduction parameter (A/V²).
\(W\)
Channel width (m).
\(\mu_n\)
Electron mobility in the inversion layer (m²/V·s; often quoted in cm²/V·s).
\(C_{ox}\)
Oxide capacitance per unit area (F/m²).
\(L\)
Channel length (m).
\(k_n'\)
Process conduction parameter, fixed by the fabrication process (A/V²).
\(\varepsilon_{ox}\)
Oxide permittivity, \(3.9\varepsilon_0\) for silicon dioxide (F/m).
\(t_{ox}\)
Oxide thickness (m).

The designer controls \(W/L\); the process fixes \(k_n'\). In the ideal model the saturation current does not depend on \(v_{DS}\) at all, so the output resistance \(r_o = \Delta v_{DS}/\Delta i_D\) is infinite.

Figure 2.1: Computed for \(K_n = 0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2\) and \(V_{TN} = 1\ \text{V}\). Each teal curve rises as a parabola in the non-saturation region and goes flat at \(v_{DS(sat)} = v_{GS} - V_{TN}\). The dashed amber curve, \(i_D = K_n v_{DS}^2\), joins all those corners.

Worked example 2.1 conduction parameter from the device

An NMOS has \(V_{TN} = 0.5\ \text{V}\), \(W = 30\ \mu\text{m}\), \(L = 1\ \mu\text{m}\), \(\mu_n = 600\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V·s}\), \(t_{ox} = 250\ \text{Å}\), \(\varepsilon_{ox} = (3.9)(8.85\times10^{-14})\ \text{F/cm}\). Find \(K_n\) and the saturation current for \(v_{GS} = 1\ \text{V}\) and 2 V.

1
Work in centimetres: \[ C_{ox} = \dfrac{(3.9)(8.85\times10^{-14})}{250\times10^{-8}} = 1.381\times10^{-7}\ \text{F/cm}^2 \]
2
\[ K_n = \dfrac{(30\times10^{-4})(600)(1.381\times10^{-7})}{2(1\times10^{-4})} = 1.24\ \text{mA/V}^2 \]
3
\(v_{GS} = 1\ \text{V}\): \(i_D = 1.24(0.5)^2 = 0.311\ \text{mA}\). \(v_{GS} = 2\ \text{V}\): \(i_D = 1.24(1.5)^2 = 2.80\ \text{mA}\).
\(K_n = 1.24\ \text{mA/V}^2\); \(i_D = 0.311\ \text{mA}\) and \(2.80\ \text{mA}\). Doubling \(v_{GS}\) here multiplies the current by 9, because it triples \(v_{GS} - V_{TN}\).
Worked example 2.2 predicting the current from one measurement

An NMOS with \(V_{TN} = 0.8\ \text{V}\) carries 1.2 mA at \(v_{GS} = 2.8\ \text{V}\), \(v_{DS} = 5\ \text{V}\). Find \(i_D\) at (a) \(v_{GS} = 1.8\ \text{V}\), \(v_{DS} = 5\ \text{V}\), and (b) \(v_{GS} = 2.8\ \text{V}\), \(v_{DS} = 1.2\ \text{V}\).

1
The measurement: \(v_{DS(sat)} = 2.0\ \text{V} \lt 5\ \text{V}\), so it was in saturation. \(K_n = 1.2/(2.0)^2 = 0.30\ \text{mA/V}^2\).
2
(a) \(v_{DS(sat)} = 1.0\ \text{V} \lt 5\ \text{V}\): saturation, \(i_D = 0.30(1.0)^2 = 0.30\ \text{mA}\).
3
(b) \(v_{DS(sat)} = 2.0\ \text{V} \gt 1.2\ \text{V}\): non-saturation, \(i_D = 0.30[2(2.0)(1.2) - 1.2^2] = 1.01\ \text{mA}\).
(a) 0.30 mA. (b) 1.01 mA. Always decide the region before choosing the equation.

03PMOS and circuit symbols

Reading Neamen 3.1.4 to 3.1.6; Lecture 23

PMOS characteristics

The p-channel enhancement MOSFET has an n-type substrate and p-type source and drain. Holes are the carriers, so a negative gate voltage (relative to the source) is needed to form the hole inversion layer. Its threshold \(V_{TP}\) is negative, it turns on when \(v_{SG} \ge |V_{TP}|\), and conventional current flows into the source and out of the drain. Writing the equations with \(v_{SG}\) and \(v_{SD}\) keeps every quantity positive.

PMOS drain current\[ \text{non-saturation } (v_{SD} \lt v_{SD(sat)}):\quad i_D = K_p\left[2(v_{SG} + V_{TP})v_{SD} - v_{SD}^2\right] \] \[ \text{saturation } (v_{SD} \ge v_{SD(sat)}):\quad i_D = K_p(v_{SG} + V_{TP})^2 \qquad v_{SD(sat)} = v_{SG} + V_{TP} \]
\(v_{SD}\)
Source-drain voltage (V).
\(v_{SD(sat)}\)
Source-drain voltage at the saturation boundary (V).
\(i_D\)
Drain current, flowing out of the drain (A).
\(K_p\)
Conduction parameter, \(\frac{W\mu_p C_{ox}}{2L}\) with hole mobility \(\mu_p\) (A/V²).
\(v_{SG}\)
Source-gate voltage (V).
\(V_{TP}\)
Threshold voltage; negative for an enhancement PMOS (V).
Watch out

Note the plus sign: \(v_{SG} + V_{TP}\). With \(V_{TP} = -0.5\ \text{V}\) and \(v_{SG} = 2\ \text{V}\), the overdrive is \(2 + (-0.5) = 1.5\ \text{V}\). Writing \(v_{SG} - V_{TP}\) is the most common PMOS mistake.

Worked example 3.1 source-drain voltage for saturation

A PMOS has \(K_p = 0.4\ \text{mA/V}^2\), \(V_{TP} = -0.7\ \text{V}\) and must carry \(i_D = 0.9\ \text{mA}\) in saturation. What \(v_{SG}\) is needed, and how large must \(v_{SD}\) be?

1
\(0.9 = 0.4(v_{SG} - 0.7)^2\), so \(v_{SG} - 0.7 = 1.5\) and \(v_{SG} = 2.2\ \text{V}\).
2
\(v_{SD(sat)} = 2.2 + (-0.7) = 1.5\ \text{V}\).
\(v_{SG} = 2.2\ \text{V}\), and \(v_{SD}\) must be at least 1.5 V.
Worked example 3.2 PMOS from one measurement

A PMOS with \(V_{TP} = -1.0\ \text{V}\) carries 0.8 mA at \(v_{SG} = 3\ \text{V}\), \(v_{SD} = 4\ \text{V}\). Find \(i_D\) for (a) \(v_{SG} = 2\ \text{V}\), \(v_{SD} = 3\ \text{V}\), and (b) \(v_{SG} = 4\ \text{V}\), \(v_{SD} = 1.5\ \text{V}\).

1
\(v_{SD(sat)} = 2\ \text{V} \lt 4\ \text{V}\): the measurement is in saturation, so \(K_p = 0.8/(2)^2 = 0.2\ \text{mA/V}^2\).
2
(a) \(v_{SD(sat)} = 1\ \text{V} \lt 3\ \text{V}\): \(i_D = 0.2(1)^2 = 0.2\ \text{mA}\).
3
(b) \(v_{SD(sat)} = 3\ \text{V} \gt 1.5\ \text{V}\): \(i_D = 0.2[2(3)(1.5) - 1.5^2] = 1.35\ \text{mA}\).
(a) 0.20 mA. (b) 1.35 mA.
Worked example 3.3 which region?

A PMOS has \(V_{TP} = -0.5\ \text{V}\). Find its region when (a) \(V_S = V_G = 1.5\ \text{V}\); (b) \(V_S = 3\ \text{V}\), \(V_G = 1\ \text{V}\), \(V_D = 0\); (c) \(V_S = 3\ \text{V}\), \(V_G = 1\ \text{V}\), \(V_D = 2\ \text{V}\).

1
(a) \(v_{SG} = 0 \lt 0.5\ \text{V}\): cutoff.
2
(b) \(v_{SG} = 2\ \text{V}\), \(v_{SD(sat)} = 1.5\ \text{V}\), \(v_{SD} = 3\ \text{V} \gt 1.5\ \text{V}\): saturation.
3
(c) Same \(v_{SG}\), but \(v_{SD} = 1\ \text{V} \lt 1.5\ \text{V}\): non-saturation.
(a) cutoff, (b) saturation, (c) non-saturation.

Symbols and conventions

In this course the source and substrate (body) are assumed to be connected. The arrow is on the source and shows the direction of current: out of the source for NMOS (current flows into the drain), into the source for PMOS (current flows out of the drain). The vertical line is the gate, separated from the channel line by the oxide gap. A broken channel line means enhancement mode; a solid line means a channel exists at zero bias (depletion mode).

Figure 3.1: NMOS devices are drawn with the source at the bottom and PMOS devices with the source at the top, so that current flows downwards in both. The textbook also uses a three-terminal form with an explicit body (B) connection.

04Depletion mode, CMOS and the summary

Reading Neamen 3.1.7 and 3.1.8; Lecture 24

In a depletion-mode MOSFET a channel exists at \(v_{GS} = 0\). For an n-channel device, a negative \(v_{GS}\) creates a space-charge region that narrows the channel, and at \(v_{GS} = V_{TN}\) (negative) the channel is fully depleted and the current is zero. A positive \(v_{GS}\) forms an electron accumulation layer and increases the current.

The current equations are exactly the same as for enhancement devices. Only the sign of the threshold changes:

NMOSPMOS
Enhancement\(V_{TN} \gt 0\)\(V_{TP} \lt 0\)
Depletion\(V_{TN} \lt 0\)\(V_{TP} \gt 0\)

Figure 4.1: Both curves are \(i_D = K_n(v_{GS} - V_{TN})^2\). The enhancement device (\(K_n = 0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2\)) starts conducting at +1 V. The depletion device (\(K_n = 0.8\ \text{mA/V}^2\)) already carries 3.2 mA at \(v_{GS} = 0\) (dot) and only turns off at −2 V.

CMOS (complementary MOS) puts n-channel and p-channel devices on the same chip, the PMOS in an n-type well.

A real transistor's saturation current rises slightly with \(v_{DS}\) because the effective channel shortens. The summary slide includes this as \(i_D = K_n(v_{GS} - V_{TN})^2(1 + \lambda v_{DS})\). With \(\lambda = 0\) (the ideal case used in this week's problems) the output resistance is infinite.

Worked example 4.1 depletion NMOS currents

A depletion NMOS has \(V_{TN} = -2\ \text{V}\) and \(K_n = 0.8\ \text{mA/V}^2\). Find \(i_D\) at \(v_{GS} = 0\) for \(v_{DS} = 0.5\), 2 and 4 V, and at \(v_{GS} = -1\ \text{V}\), \(v_{DS} = 4\ \text{V}\).

1
At \(v_{GS} = 0\): \(v_{DS(sat)} = 0 - (-2) = 2\ \text{V}\).
2
\(v_{DS} = 0.5\ \text{V}\) (non-saturation): \(i_D = 0.8[2(2)(0.5) - 0.25] = 1.40\ \text{mA}\).
3
\(v_{DS} = 2\ \text{V}\) is exactly at the boundary, where both equations give \(0.8(2)^2 = 3.20\ \text{mA}\); \(v_{DS} = 4\ \text{V}\) is saturated with the same 3.20 mA.
4
\(v_{GS} = -1\ \text{V}\): overdrive \(-1 + 2 = 1\ \text{V}\), saturated at \(v_{DS} = 4\ \text{V}\): \(i_D = 0.8(1)^2 = 0.80\ \text{mA}\).
1.40 mA, 3.20 mA, 3.20 mA and 0.80 mA.
Worked example 4.2 width-to-length ratio of a depletion NMOS

A depletion NMOS has \(V_{TN} = -1.5\ \text{V}\) and \(k_n' = 100\ \mu\text{A/V}^2\). It carries 0.9 mA at \(v_{GS} = 0\), \(v_{DS} = 3\ \text{V}\). Find \(W/L\).

1
\(v_{DS(sat)} = 1.5\ \text{V} \lt 3\ \text{V}\): saturation.
2
\(K_n = 0.9/(1.5)^2 = 0.40\ \text{mA/V}^2\); \(W/L = 2K_n/k_n' = 2(0.40)/0.100 = 8.0\).
\(W/L = 8.0\).
Worked example 4.3 depletion PMOS

A depletion PMOS has \(V_{TP} = +1.5\ \text{V}\), \(k_p' = 50\ \mu\text{A/V}^2\) and \(W/L = 8\). Find \(v_{SD(sat)}\) and the saturation current for \(v_{SG} = -1\), 0 and +1 V.

1
\(K_p = \frac{50}{2}(8) = 0.20\ \text{mA/V}^2\).
2
\(v_{SG} = -1\ \text{V}\): \(v_{SD(sat)} = 0.5\ \text{V}\), \(i_D = 0.2(0.5)^2 = 0.050\ \text{mA}\).
3
\(v_{SG} = 0\): \(v_{SD(sat)} = 1.5\ \text{V}\), \(i_D = 0.45\ \text{mA}\). \(v_{SG} = +1\ \text{V}\): \(v_{SD(sat)} = 2.5\ \text{V}\), \(i_D = 1.25\ \text{mA}\).
\(v_{SD(sat)} = 0.5\), 1.5, 2.5 V; \(i_D = 0.050\), 0.45, 1.25 mA. The device conducts even with \(v_{SG}\) slightly negative.

05Common-source DC analysis

Reading Neamen 3.2.1; Lecture 25

In the common-source circuit the source is common to input and output. Because the gate current is zero, the gate divider is unloaded: \(V_G\) follows directly from \(R_1\) and \(R_2\). The method mirrors the BJT one: assume saturation, solve, check \(v_{DS} \ge v_{DS(sat)}\); if the check fails, redo it in the non-saturation region.

NMOS common source\[ V_{GS} = V_G = \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}V_{DD} \qquad V_{DS} = V_{DD} - I_D R_D \qquad P_T = I_D V_{DS} \]
\(V_{GS}\)
Gate-source voltage (V); equal to \(V_G\) because the source is grounded.
\(V_G\)
Gate voltage set by the divider (V).
\(R_2\)
Divider resistor, gate to ground (\(\Omega\)).
\(R_1\)
Divider resistor, supply to gate (\(\Omega\)).
\(V_{DD}\)
Supply voltage (V).
\(V_{DS}\)
Drain-source voltage (V).
\(I_D\)
Drain current (A).
\(R_D\)
Drain resistor (\(\Omega\)).
\(P_T\)
Power dissipated in the transistor (W).

NMOS common source

Figure 5.1: No current flows into the gate, so \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) form a plain voltage divider. The drain current flows from \(V_{DD}\) through \(R_D\).

Worked example 5.1 NMOS operating point

In Figure 5.1, \(V_{DD} = 6\ \text{V}\), \(R_1 = 40\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_2 = 20\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_D = 10\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(V_{TN} = 1\ \text{V}\), \(K_n = 0.25\ \text{mA/V}^2\). Find \(I_D\), \(V_{DS}\) and \(P_T\).

1
\(V_{GS} = \frac{20}{60}(6) = 2\ \text{V}\).
2
Assume saturation: \(I_D = 0.25(2 - 1)^2 = 0.25\ \text{mA}\).
3
\(V_{DS} = 6 - (0.25)(10) = 3.5\ \text{V}\); \(V_{DS(sat)} = 1\ \text{V}\). Since \(3.5 \gt 1\), the assumption holds.
4
\(P_T = (0.25\ \text{mA})(3.5\ \text{V}) = 0.875\ \text{mW}\).
\(I_D = 0.25\ \text{mA}\), \(V_{DS} = 3.5\ \text{V}\), \(P_T = 0.875\ \text{mW}\).
Worked example 5.2 low-voltage NMOS

Repeat for \(V_{DD} = 3.3\ \text{V}\), \(R_1 = 400\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_2 = 200\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_D = 40\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(V_{TN} = 0.4\ \text{V}\), \(K_n = 50\ \mu\text{A/V}^2\).

1
\(V_{GS} = \frac{200}{600}(3.3) = 1.10\ \text{V}\).
2
Saturation: \(I_D = 50(0.70)^2 = 24.5\ \mu\text{A}\); \(V_{DS} = 3.3 - (24.5\ \mu\text{A})(40\ \text{k}\Omega) = 2.32\ \text{V} \gt 0.70\ \text{V}\). Confirmed.
\(I_D = 24.5\ \mu\text{A}\), \(V_{GS} = 1.10\ \text{V}\), \(V_{DS} = 2.32\ \text{V}\).

PMOS and the triode check

For a PMOS common-source stage the source is at \(V_{DD}\), so \(V_{SG} = V_{DD} - V_G\) and \(V_{SD} = V_{DD} - I_D R_D\).

Figure 5.2: The gate is pulled below the source by the divider. The drain current flows out of the drain, through \(R_D\) to ground.

Worked example 5.3 when the saturation guess fails

In Figure 5.2, \(V_{DD} = 5\ \text{V}\), \(R_1 = R_2 = 100\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_D = 10\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(V_{TP} = -1\ \text{V}\), \(K_p = 0.3\ \text{mA/V}^2\). Find \(I_D\) and \(V_{SD}\).

1
\(V_G = 2.5\ \text{V}\), so \(V_{SG} = 2.5\ \text{V}\) and \(V_{SD(sat)} = 2.5 - 1 = 1.5\ \text{V}\).
2
Assume saturation: \(I_D = 0.3(1.5)^2 = 0.675\ \text{mA}\), giving \(V_{SD} = 5 - 6.75 = -1.75\ \text{V}\). Impossible, so the transistor is in non-saturation.
3
Substitute \(V_{SD} = 5 - 10I_D\) (mA, kΩ) into \(I_D = 0.3[2(1.5)V_{SD} - V_{SD}^2]\). This is a quadratic with roots \(V_{SD} = 0.613\ \text{V}\) (\(I_D = 0.439\ \text{mA}\)) and \(V_{SD} = 2.72\ \text{V}\) (\(I_D = 0.228\ \text{mA}\)).
4
The second root has \(V_{SD} \gt V_{SD(sat)} = 1.5\ \text{V}\), which contradicts the non-saturation equation used to find it. Reject it.
\(I_D = 0.439\ \text{mA}\), \(V_{SD} = 0.613\ \text{V}\), non-saturation region.
Exam

A non-saturation analysis always gives two roots. Show the check that rejects one: substitute it back and show it falls outside the region whose equation produced it, as Lecture 25 does for Example 3.4.

Designing a bias

Worked example 5.4 PMOS design for a given Q-point

Design Figure 5.2 for \(V_{DD} = 5\ \text{V}\), \(I_{DQ} = 0.4\ \text{mA}\), \(V_{SDQ} = 2.5\ \text{V}\) and \(R_1 \parallel R_2 = 250\ \text{k}\Omega\). \(V_{TP} = -0.8\ \text{V}\), \(K_p = 0.25\ \text{mA/V}^2\).

1
\(R_D = (5 - 2.5)/0.4\ \text{mA} = 6.25\ \text{k}\Omega\).
2
Saturation: \(0.4 = 0.25(V_{SG} - 0.8)^2\), so \(V_{SG} = 0.8 + 1.265 = 2.065\ \text{V}\). Check: \(V_{SD(sat)} = 1.265\ \text{V} \lt 2.5\ \text{V}\).
3
\(V_G = 5 - 2.065 = 2.935\ \text{V}\). Since \(V_G = \frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}V_{DD} = \frac{R_1 \parallel R_2}{R_1}V_{DD}\): \(R_1 = (250)(5)/2.935 = 426\ \text{k}\Omega\) and \(R_2 = (250)(5)/(5 - 2.935) = 605\ \text{k}\Omega\).
\(R_D = 6.25\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_1 = 426\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_2 = 605\ \text{k}\Omega\).

\(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2} = 1.207/2.5 = 0.4828\); \(R_1 = 150/0.4828 = 311\ \text{k}\Omega\); \(R_2 = 150/0.5172 = 290\ \text{k}\Omega\).
3
Check: \(V_{DS} = 2.5 - 1 = 1.5\ \text{V} \gt V_{DS(sat)} = 0.707\ \text{V}\).
\(R_1 = 311\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_2 = 290\ \text{k}\Omega\).
Problem 6.3 PMOS with a source resistor

A PMOS has \(R_S = 1\ \text{k}\Omega\) from +4 V to its source and \(R_D = 4\ \text{k}\Omega\) from its drain to −4 V. Its gate is set by \(R_1 = 10\ \text{k}\Omega\) to +4 V and \(R_2 = 30\ \text{k}\Omega\) to −4 V. \(K_p = 0.4\ \text{mA/V}^2\), \(V_{TP} = -1\ \text{V}\). Find \(I_D\), \(V_{SG}\) and \(V_{SD}\).

1
\(V_G = \frac{30}{40}(8) - 4 = 2\ \text{V}\).
2
Loop from +4 V: \(4 = I_D R_S + V_{SG} + V_G\), with \(I_D = 0.4(V_{SG} - 1)^2\): \(0.4(V_{SG} - 1)^2 + V_{SG} - 2 = 0\).
3
Roots: \(V_{SG} = 1.766\ \text{V}\) or \(-2.27\ \text{V}\). The second is below \(|V_{TP}|\), so the transistor would be off: reject it.
4
\(I_D = 0.4(0.766)^2 = 0.234\ \text{mA}\); \(V_{SD} = 8 - (0.234)(5) = 6.83\ \text{V} \gt 0.766\ \text{V}\): saturation confirmed.
\(I_D = 0.234\ \text{mA}\), \(V_{SG} = 1.77\ \text{V}\), \(V_{SD} = 6.83\ \text{V}\).
Problem 6.4 depletion PMOS with limited bias current

A depletion PMOS (\(V_{TP} = +1\ \text{V}\), \(K_p = 0.4\ \text{mA/V}^2\)) has \(R_S = 2.5\ \text{k}\Omega\) from +5 V to its source and its drain grounded. The gate divider runs from +5 V (\(R_1\)) to −5 V (\(R_2\)). Design for \(V_{SD} = 3\ \text{V}\) with a divider current of 10 % of \(I_D\).

1
\(I_D = (5 - 3)/2.5\ \text{k} = 0.8\ \text{mA}\).
2
Saturation: \(V_{SG} = \pm\sqrt{0.8/0.4} - 1 = 0.414\ \text{V}\) or \(-2.41\ \text{V}\). The second makes \(V_{SG} + V_{TP}\) negative (off), so \(V_{SG} = 0.414\ \text{V}\). Check: \(V_{SD(sat)} = 1.414\ \text{V} \lt 3\ \text{V}\).
3
\(V_G = 5 - 2 - 0.414 = 2.586\ \text{V}\). Divider current 0.08 mA: \(R_1 + R_2 = 10/0.08 = 125\ \text{k}\Omega\).
4
\(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2} = (2.586 + 5)/10 = 0.7586\): \(R_2 = 94.8\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_1 = 30.2\ \text{k}\Omega\).
\(R_1 = 30.2\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_2 = 94.8\ \text{k}\Omega\).
Problem 6.5 PMOS design with split supplies

A PMOS has its source grounded and \(R_D\) from its drain to −10 V; its gate divider runs from +5 V (\(R_1\)) to −5 V (\(R_2\)). \(V_{TP} = -1.5\ \text{V}\), \(K_p = 2\ \text{mA/V}^2\). Design for \(I_D = 4\ \text{mA}\), \(V_{SD} = 5\ \text{V}\) and \(R_1 \parallel R_2 = 100\ \text{k}\Omega\).

1
\(V_D = -5\ \text{V}\), so \(R_D = (-5 + 10)/4\ \text{mA} = 1.25\ \text{k}\Omega\).
2
\(V_{SG} = \sqrt{4/2} + 1.5 = 2.914\ \text{V}\); \(V_{SD(sat)} = 1.414\ \text{V} \lt 5\ \text{V}\). \(V_G = -2.914\ \text{V}\).
3
\(\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2} = (-2.914 + 5)/10 = 0.2086\): \(R_1 = 100/0.2086 = 479\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_2 = 100/0.7914 = 126\ \text{k}\Omega\).
\(R_D = 1.25\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_1 = 479\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(R_2 = 126\ \text{k}\Omega\).
Problem 6.6 two diode-connected NMOS in series

Two NMOS transistors, each with gate tied to drain, are stacked between \(V_{DD} = 4\ \text{V}\) and ground; \(V_O\) is their junction. \(V_{TN} = 0.5\ \text{V}\), \(k_n' = 100\ \mu\text{A/V}^2\), \((W/L)_1 = 10\) (top), \((W/L)_2 = 40\) (bottom). Find \(V_{GS1}\), \(V_{GS2}\), \(V_O\) and \(I_D\), and show both are saturated.

1
\(K_{n1} = 0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2\), \(K_{n2} = 2\ \text{mA/V}^2\); \(V_{GS1} + V_{GS2} = 4\ \text{V}\).
2
Same current: \(0.5(V_{GS1} - 0.5)^2 = 2(3.5 - V_{GS1})^2\), so \(V_{GS1} - 0.5 = 2(3.5 - V_{GS1})\) and \(V_{GS1} = 2.5\ \text{V}\).
3
\(V_{GS2} = 1.5\ \text{V} = V_O\); \(I_D = 0.5(2.0)^2 = 2.0\ \text{mA}\).
4
Each has \(V_{DS} = V_{GS}\), which is always greater than \(V_{DS(sat)} = V_{GS} - V_{TN}\), so a diode-connected MOSFET is always saturated when on.
\(V_{GS1} = 2.5\ \text{V}\), \(V_{GS2} = V_O = 1.5\ \text{V}\), \(I_D = 2.0\ \text{mA}\).
Problem 6.7 PMOS LED switch

A PMOS with its source at +5 V switches an LED (\(V_\gamma = 1.8\ \text{V}\)) in series with \(R_D\) to ground. \(V_{TP} = -0.7\ \text{V}\), \(k_p' = 50\ \mu\text{A/V}^2\), \(\lambda = 0\). Design \(R_D\) and \(W/L\) for \(I_D = 20\ \text{mA}\) with \(V_{SD} = 0.25\ \text{V}\) when \(V_I = 0\).

1
\(V_{SG} = 5\ \text{V}\), so \(V_{SD(sat)} = 4.3\ \text{V} \gt 0.25\ \text{V}\): the switch is in non-saturation, as a closed switch should be.
2
\(R_D = (5 - 0.25 - 1.8)/20\ \text{mA} = 147.5\ \Omega\).
3
\(K_p = \dfrac{20}{2(4.3)(0.25) - 0.25^2} = 9.58\ \text{mA/V}^2\); \(W/L = 2(9.58)/0.050 = 383\).
\(R_D = 148\ \Omega\), \(W/L = 383\): a large ratio is the price of a low on-resistance.

07Formula sheet

NMOS

\(v_{DS(sat)} = v_{GS} - V_{TN}\)

Non-sat: \(i_D = K_n[2(v_{GS} - V_{TN})v_{DS} - v_{DS}^2]\)

Sat: \(i_D = K_n(v_{GS} - V_{TN})^2\)

PMOS

\(v_{SD(sat)} = v_{SG} + V_{TP}\)

Non-sat: \(i_D = K_p[2(v_{SG} + V_{TP})v_{SD} - v_{SD}^2]\)

Sat: \(i_D = K_p(v_{SG} + V_{TP})^2\)

Parameters

\(K_n = \frac{k_n'}{2}\frac{W}{L} = \frac{W\mu_n C_{ox}}{2L}\)

\(k_n' = \mu_n C_{ox}\), \(C_{ox} = \varepsilon_{ox}/t_{ox}\)

With \(\lambda\): multiply the saturation current by \((1 + \lambda v_{DS})\)

Threshold signs

Enhancement: \(V_{TN} \gt 0\), \(V_{TP} \lt 0\)

Depletion: \(V_{TN} \lt 0\), \(V_{TP} \gt 0\)

Common source

\(V_G = \frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}V_{DD}\) (no gate current)

NMOS: \(V_{DS} = V_{DD} - I_D R_D\); PMOS: \(V_{SG} = V_{DD} - V_G\)

\(P_T = I_D V_{DS}\)
