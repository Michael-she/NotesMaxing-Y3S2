ELECTRONICS 245 · CHAPTERS 3 AND 4 · DIODES IN ACTIVE CIRCUITS

Diode-connected MOSFETs

What happens when the gate of an enhancement MOSFET is tied to its drain, why the device then acts as a non-linear resistor, and how to find its operating point and its small-signal resistance.

01The diode connection

Reading Chapters 3 and 4 summary, section 3.1

In discrete and integrated circuits an enhancement-mode NMOS transistor is often used with its gate wired directly to its drain. The device then has only two terminals, drain and source, and behaves like a diode: it conducts in one direction only, and it needs a minimum voltage before it conducts.

Figure 1.1: The gate wire forces \(v_{GS} = v_{DS}\). The current is zero until the voltage reaches \(V_{TN}\) and then rises as a parabola. The dashed tangent at the operating point Q has slope \(g_m\). The curve is drawn for \(K_n = 0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2\) and \(V_{TN} = 1\ \text{V}\).

A transistor is in saturation when \(v_{DS} \ge v_{GS} - V_{TN}\). With the gate tied to the drain, \(v_{DS} = v_{GS}\), so the condition becomes \(v_{GS} \ge v_{GS} - V_{TN}\), which is \(V_{TN} \ge 0\). It holds for every enhancement device. Whenever the transistor conducts, it is in saturation.

Diode-connected NMOS\[ i_D = K_n\,(v_{GS} - V_{TN})^2 = K_n\,(v_{DS} - V_{TN})^2 \qquad (v_{DS} \gt V_{TN}) \] \[ i_D = 0 \qquad (v_{DS} \le V_{TN}) \]
\(i_D\)
Drain current (A).
\(K_n\)
Conduction parameter (A/V\(^2\)).
\(v_{GS}\)
Gate-source voltage (V).
\(v_{DS}\)
Drain-source voltage, equal to \(v_{GS}\) here (V).
\(V_{TN}\)
Threshold voltage (V).
Watch out

The summary says the connection "forces \(v_{DS} \gt v_{DS}(\text{sat})\)". The inequality is strict only when \(V_{TN} \gt 0\), which is true for an enhancement device. The summary's equation also omits the case below threshold: the current is zero, not negative, for \(v_{DS} \lt V_{TN}\).

Key idea

A diode-connected MOSFET is a two-terminal, non-linear resistor with a built-in threshold. Because the transistor is an active device, the "diode" can be made to any \(K_n\) by choosing the aspect ratio, which is why integrated circuits use it as an enhancement load.

02DC solution with a resistor

Reading Chapters 3 and 4 summary, section 3.1, with Week 10 and Week 11 notes

To find the operating point, combine the transistor equation with the circuit equation. For a resistor \(R\) from \(V_{DD}\) to the drain:

Operating point\[ \frac{V_{DD} - V}{R} = K_n\,(V - V_{TN})^2 \] \[ u = V - V_{TN}: \qquad K_nR\,u^2 + u - (V_{DD} - V_{TN}) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4K_nR\,(V_{DD} - V_{TN})}}{2K_nR} \]
\(V\)
Drain voltage, equal to the gate voltage (V).
\(V_{DD}\)
Supply voltage (V).
\(R\)
Series resistor (\(\Omega\)).
\(K_n\)
Conduction parameter (A/V\(^2\)).
\(V_{TN}\)
Threshold voltage (V).
\(u\)
Overdrive voltage \(V - V_{TN}\) (V).

The quadratic has two roots. Keep the one with \(u \gt 0\), because the other puts the transistor below threshold where the equation no longer applies.

Figure 2.1: The dashed straight line is \(I = (V_{DD} - V)/R\). The teal curve is the transistor. Their single crossing Q gives the voltage and current together.

Worked example 2.1 an enhancement load

In Figure 2.1, \(V_{DD} = 5\ \text{V}\), \(R = 2\ \text{k}\Omega\), \(K_n = 0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2\) and \(V_{TN} = 1\ \text{V}\). Find \(V\) and \(I\), and check that the transistor is in saturation.

1
\(K_nR = (0.5\times10^{-3})(2000) = 1.0\), so \(u^2 + u - 4 = 0\).
2
\(u = (-1 + \sqrt{17})/2 = (-1 + 4.123)/2 = 1.5616\ \text{V}\). The other root, \(-1.56\ \text{V}\), is rejected.
3
\(V = V_{TN} + u = 2.5616\ \text{V}\).
4
\(I = K_nu^2 = (0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2)(1.5616\ \text{V})^2 = 1.219\ \text{mA}\). Check with the resistor: \((5 - 2.5616)/2000 = 1.219\ \text{mA}\).
5
Saturation: \(v_{DS} = 2.56\ \text{V}\) exceeds \(v_{GS} - V_{TN} = 1.56\ \text{V}\) by exactly \(V_{TN} = 1\ \text{V}\).
\(V = 2.56\ \text{V}\) and \(I = 1.219\ \text{mA}\). The device is in saturation.

The Week 10 notes use the same device as the "diode-connected stack" in the amplifier chain, and the Week 11 notes use it as the enhancement load of an NMOS inverter. The method above is the one those pages apply.

03Small-signal resistance

Reading Chapters 3 and 4 summary, section 3.1

For a small change about the operating point, the device looks like a resistor. Differentiate \(i_D = K_n(v_{GS} - V_{TN})^2\) with respect to \(v_{GS}\) at the operating point. The result is the transconductance \(g_m\), and the resistance seen looking into the drain is its reciprocal, because \(v_{DS}\) and \(v_{GS}\) move together.

Small-signal resistance\[ g_m = \frac{\partial i_D}{\partial v_{GS}} = 2K_n\,(V_{GS} - V_{TN}) = 2\sqrt{K_nI_D} \qquad r = \frac{1}{g_m} \]
\(g_m\)
Transconductance at the operating point (S).
\(K_n\)
Conduction parameter (A/V\(^2\)).
\(V_{GS}\)
DC gate-source voltage (V).
\(V_{TN}\)
Threshold voltage (V).
\(I_D\)
DC drain current (A).
\(r\)
Small-signal resistance looking into the drain (\(\Omega\)).

The resistance is not fixed. It falls as the current rises, because \(r = 1/(2\sqrt{K_nI_D})\). It is the slope of the dashed tangent in Figure 1.1, inverted.

Watch out

\(r = 1/g_m\) assumes no channel-length modulation. With a finite \(\lambda\) the output conductance \(g_o \approx \lambda I_D\) is in parallel, so \(r = 1/(g_m + g_o)\). The summary states the simple form, which is what this page uses.

Worked example 3.1 the load's resistance and the signal it passes

Use the operating point of example 2.1. Find \(g_m\) and \(r\). A small source \(v_s\) drives the node through the \(2\ \text{k}\Omega\) resistor, as in a divider with the device. Find \(v/v_s\) at the drain.

1
\(g_m = 2K_nu = 2(0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2)(1.5616\ \text{V}) = 1.5616\ \text{mS}\). Check: \(2\sqrt{K_nI_D} = 1.5616\ \text{mS}\).
2
\(r = 1/g_m = 1/1.5616\ \text{mS} = 640.4\ \Omega\).
3
Divider: \(v/v_s = r/(R + r) = 640.4/(2000 + 640.4) = 0.243\).
\(g_m = 1.562\ \text{mS}\), \(r = 640\ \Omega\), and the signal at the drain is \(0.243\) of the source signal.

04Against the pn diode

Reading Chapters 3 and 4 summary, section 3.1, and the Chapter 1 page

Both devices are non-linear two-terminal resistors with a small-signal resistance that falls as current rises. Their laws differ.

pn diodeDiode-connected MOSFET
Law\(i_D = I_S\left(e^{v_D/V_T} - 1\right)\)\(i_D = K_n(v_{DS} - V_{TN})^2\)
Starts to conductGradually, about \(0.5\) to \(0.7\ \text{V}\)At \(V_{TN}\), set by the process
Small-signal resistance\(r_d = V_T/I_D\)\(r = 1/(2\sqrt{K_nI_D})\)
Dependence on current\(\propto 1/I_D\)\(\propto 1/\sqrt{I_D}\)
Voltage drop at 1 mANearly fixed at about \(0.7\ \text{V}\)\(V_{TN} + \sqrt{I_D/K_n}\), design-dependent

At the same current of \(1.22\ \text{mA}\) a pn diode would have \(r_d = 26\ \text{mV}/1.22\ \text{mA} = 21.3\ \Omega\), thirty times smaller than the MOSFET's \(640\ \Omega\). The MOSFET is the softer element, which is why it works as a load and not as a precise voltage reference.

05Practice problems

Problem 5.1 a lower-voltage load

A diode-connected NMOS with \(K_n = 0.25\ \text{mA/V}^2\) and \(V_{TN} = 0.8\ \text{V}\) is connected to \(V_{DD} = 3.3\ \text{V}\) through \(R = 5\ \text{k}\Omega\). Find \(V\), \(I\), \(g_m\) and \(r\).

1
\(K_nR = 1.25\), so \(1.25u^2 + u - 2.5 = 0\) and \(u = (-1 + \sqrt{13.5})/2.5 = 1.0697\ \text{V}\).
2
\(V = 0.8 + 1.0697 = 1.870\ \text{V}\) and \(I = 0.25\ \text{mA/V}^2\times(1.0697)^2 = 286\ \mu\text{A}\).
3
\(g_m = 2(0.25)(1.0697) = 535\ \mu\text{S}\) and \(r = 1/g_m = 1.87\ \text{k}\Omega\).
\(V = 1.87\ \text{V}\), \(I = 286\ \mu\text{A}\), \(g_m = 535\ \mu\text{S}\), \(r = 1.87\ \text{k}\Omega\).
Problem 5.2 from a current to a resistance

A diode-connected NMOS with \(K_n = 0.5\ \text{mA/V}^2\) and \(V_{TN} = 1\ \text{V}\) carries \(0.4\ \text{mA}\). Find \(V_{GS}\), \(g_m\) and \(r\).

1
\(u = \sqrt{I/K_n} = \sqrt{0.4/0.5} = 0.8944\ \text{V}\), so \(V_{GS} = 1 + 0.8944 = 1.894\ \text{V}\).
2
\(g_m = 2\sqrt{K_nI} = 2\sqrt{(0.5)(0.4)} = 0.894\ \text{mS}\), and \(r = 1118\ \Omega\).
\(V_{GS} = 1.89\ \text{V}\), \(g_m = 0.894\ \text{mS}\), \(r = 1118\ \Omega\).
Problem 5.3 a divider with the device at the bottom

The device of problem 5.2 sits at the bottom of a \(10\ \text{k}\Omega\) resistor. Find the small-signal gain \(v/v_s\) at the drain.

1
\(v/v_s = r/(R + r) = 1118/(10000 + 1118) = 0.1006\).
The gain is \(0.101\), about \(-20\ \text{dB}\). A diode-connected device cannot amplify; it divides.

06Formula sheet

Diode-connected NMOS

\(v_{GS} = v_{DS}\), always in saturation when it conducts

\(i_D = K_n(v_{DS} - V_{TN})^2\) for \(v_{DS} \gt V_{TN}\), else \(0\)

Operating point with a resistor

\(u = \dfrac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4K_nR(V_{DD} - V_{TN})}}{2K_nR}\), \(V = V_{TN} + u\)

\(I = K_nu^2 = (V_{DD} - V)/R\)

Small signal

\(g_m = 2K_nu = 2\sqrt{K_nI_D}\)

\(r = 1/g_m\), or \(1/(g_m + g_o)\) with channel-length modulation

Divider: \(v/v_s = r/(R + r)\)
