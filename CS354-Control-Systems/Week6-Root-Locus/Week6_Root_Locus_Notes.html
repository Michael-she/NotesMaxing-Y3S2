The root-locus design method

Root locus form, the phase and magnitude conditions, sketching rules, and lead and lag design in the s-plane. Built from the Week 6 examples and Tutorial 4; there were no Week 6 lecture slides in the folder, so the theory follows FPE Chapter 5.

01The root locus

Weeks 4 and 5 designed on the Bode plot. The root locus (FPE Chapter 5) designs directly in the s-plane: it is the set of all paths traced by the closed-loop poles as one parameter, usually the gain \(K\), varies from 0 to ∞. Because week 1 showed how pole positions fix the transient, the locus shows at a glance which responses a controller structure can reach.

The first step is always to write the characteristic equation in root locus form, with the parameter of interest as a multiplying factor:

Root locus form (FPE 5.1)\[ 1 + KL(s) = 0, \qquad L(s) = \frac{b(s)}{a(s)} \]
\(K\)
Parameter of interest, varied from 0 to ∞ (usually a gain, but it can be a pole or zero position, as in Tutorial 4 problem 2.3).
\(L(s)\)
Loop transfer function in root locus form, with monic polynomials.
\(b(s)\)
Numerator, order \(m\); its roots are the open-loop zeros.
\(a(s)\)
Denominator, order \(n\); its roots are the open-loop poles.

For a unity-feedback loop with \(D(s) = K\) this is just \(L(s) = G(s)\). For a compensator, \(L = D(s)G(s)\) with the gain pulled out front. For another parameter, rearrange: for example \(s^2 + (2 + c)s + 4 = 0\) with \(c\) as the parameter becomes \(1 + c\,\dfrac{s}{s^2 + 2s + 4} = 0\).

02Phase and magnitude conditions

A point \(s_0\) is on the locus if some positive \(K\) makes \(1 + KL(s_0) = 0\), that is \(L(s_0) = -1/K\): a negative real number. That splits into two conditions.

Phase condition (FPE 5.6)\[ \angle L(s_0) = \sum_{i=1}^{m}\psi_i - \sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_i = 180^\circ + 360^\circ(l - 1) \]
\(\psi_i\)
Angle of the vector from zero \(z_i\) to \(s_0\), measured anticlockwise from the positive real direction.
\(\phi_i\)
Angle of the vector from pole \(p_i\) to \(s_0\).
\(l\)
Any integer: the condition means the angle is an odd multiple of 180°.
Magnitude condition\[ K = \frac{1}{|L(s_0)|} = \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}|s_0 - p_i|}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|s_0 - z_i|} \]
\(K\)
Gain that puts a closed-loop pole at \(s_0\).
\(|s_0 - p_i|\)
Distance from pole \(p_i\) to \(s_0\).
\(|s_0 - z_i|\)
Distance from zero \(z_i\) to \(s_0\).
Key idea

The phase condition decides whether the locus passes through \(s_0\). Only if it does, the magnitude condition gives the gain that places a pole there. If the phase condition fails, no gain will do it and you need a compensator (section 5).

Worked example 2.1 Testing points with the phase condition

\(L(s) = \dfrac{s+2}{s(s+4)(s+6)}\). Is \(s = -1 + j\) on the locus? Find the point on the locus with real part −4.25 and the gain that puts a pole there.

1
At \(-1 + j\): \(\psi_1 = \angle(1 + j) = 45^\circ\); \(\phi_1 = \angle(-1 + j) = 135^\circ\), \(\phi_2 = \angle(3 + j) = 18.4^\circ\), \(\phi_3 = \angle(5 + j) = 11.3^\circ\). \(\angle L = 45 - 135 - 18.4 - 11.3 = -119.7^\circ\). Not an odd multiple of 180°, so not on the locus.
2
Search along \(\mathrm{Re}(s) = -4.25\) for the height where \(\angle L = -180^\circ\) (by trial, or numerically): \(s_0 = -4.25 + 5.19j\).
3
Magnitude condition: \(K = \dfrac{|s_0|\,|s_0 + 4|\,|s_0 + 6|}{|s_0 + 2|} = 33.8\).
\(-1 + j\) is not on the locus; \(-4.25 \pm 5.19j\) is, with \(K = 33.8\).

Example 1: \(\angle L(-1 + 2j) = -129.1^\circ\) for \(L = (s+1)/(s(s+5)[(s+2)^2+4])\), matching the slide's −129.2°: not on the locus. Example 2: at the rounded point \(s_0 = -0.68 + 0.58j\), \(\angle L = -175.2^\circ\), close to −180° (the test point is rounded to two decimals), and \(1/|L(s_0)| = 9.49\). 03Sketching rules

The slides for this chapter were not in your folder, so this section follows FPE 5.2. The rules let you sketch a locus by hand quickly, which is what the tests expect; MATLAB's rlocus confirms it.

  1. Start and end. There are \(n\) branches. They start at the poles of \(L\) (\(K = 0\)) and end at the zeros of \(L\) (\(K \to \infty\)), with \(n - m\) branches going to infinity.
  2. Real axis. A point on the real axis is on the locus if it lies to the left of an odd number of real poles and zeros.
  3. Asymptotes. The \(n - m\) branches to infinity follow straight lines from the centroid \(\alpha\) at angles \(\phi_l\):
Asymptotes (rule 3)\[ \alpha = \frac{\sum p_i - \sum z_i}{n - m}, \qquad \phi_l = \frac{180^\circ + 360^\circ(l - 1)}{n - m}, \quad l = 1, 2, \ldots, n - m \]
\(\alpha\)
Centroid of the asymptotes on the real axis.
\(p_i,\,z_i\)
Poles and zeros of \(L\).
\(n - m\)
Number of branches going to infinity.
\(\phi_l\)
Asymptote angles.
  1. Departure and arrival angles. Apply the phase condition to a point very close to a complex pole (or zero): the angle at which the branch leaves the pole is what makes the total angle 180°.
  2. Imaginary-axis crossings. Substitute \(s = j\omega\) into the characteristic equation and set the real and imaginary parts to zero: the two equations give the frequency and the gain where branches cross into the RHP.
  3. Break-away points. Where two branches on the real axis meet and leave, \(\dfrac{d}{ds}\left[\dfrac{a(s)}{b(s)}\right] = 0\).

Figure 3.1: Three poles, no zeros: three asymptotes at ±60° and 180° from the centroid \((0 - 2 - 5)/3 = -7/3\). The real-axis segments are \([-2, 0]\) and \((-\infty, -5]\). The branches from 0 and −2 meet at the break-away point \(-0.880\) (root of \(3s^2 + 14s + 10 = 0\)). Substituting \(s = j\omega\) into \(s^3 + 7s^2 + 10s + K = 0\) gives the imaginary-axis crossing at \(K = 70\), \(s = \pm j\sqrt{10}\).

04Lead compensation by root locus

If the phase condition fails at the desired pole \(s_0\), add a compensator so that it holds. A lead adds positive angle:

Lead compensator, root locus form (FPE 5.4)\[ D(s) = K\frac{s + z}{s + p}, \qquad p \gt z \]
\(K\)
Gain, found last from the magnitude condition.
\(z\)
Zero location (the zero is at \(-z\)).
\(p\)
Pole location (at \(-p\)), further left than the zero.

The net angle the lead adds at \(s_0\) is \(\psi_z - \phi_p \gt 0\). The course's first-iteration rules: place the zero around the natural frequency of the desired pole (or just below it), and the pole roughly 5 to 20 times further left (the class example uses a decade). Then tune \(p\) (or \(z\)) so the phase condition holds exactly, and finish with the magnitude condition for \(K\).

Worked example 4.1 Placing poles with a lead

\(G(s) = \dfrac{10}{s(s+1)}\). Design a lead so the dominant closed-loop poles are at \(s_0 = -2 \pm 2j\) (\(\zeta = 0.707\), \(\omega_n = 2.83\) rad/s).

1
Root locus form with proportional control: \(L = G\). \(\angle G(s_0) = -\angle(-2+2j) - \angle(-1+2j) = -135^\circ - 116.6^\circ = -251.6^\circ\). Not −180°, so P control cannot do it.
2
Angle the lead must add: \(-180^\circ - (-251.6^\circ) = 71.57^\circ\).
3
Zero at \(z = 2\) (just below \(\omega_n\)): \(\psi_z = \angle(s_0 + 2) = \angle(2j) = 90^\circ\).
4
The pole must contribute \(\phi_p = 90^\circ - 71.57^\circ = 18.43^\circ\): \(\tan\phi_p = \dfrac{2}{p - 2}\), so \(p = 2 + 2/\tan(18.43^\circ) = 8.00\).
5
Magnitude condition on \(L = 10\dfrac{s+2}{s(s+1)(s+8)}\): \(K = \dfrac{|s_0|\,|s_0+1|\,|s_0+8|}{10\,|s_0+2|} = \dfrac{(2.83)(2.24)(6.32)}{10 \times 2} = 2.00\).
\(D(s) = 2.00\,\dfrac{s+2}{s+8}\). The closed-loop poles are \(-2 \pm 2j\) and a third pole at \(-5.00\), far enough left for \(-2 \pm 2j\) to dominate. \(K_v = 10 \times 2.00 \times 2/8 = 5.00\) s⁻¹.

Figure 4.1: The dots are the closed-loop poles at the designed gain. Without the lead the branches from 0 and −1 break away at −0.5 and run straight up, never reaching −2 ± 2j. The lead's zero and pole bend the locus left through the target.

05Lag compensation by root locus

When proportional control already gives the right transient (the phase condition holds at \(s_0\)) but the steady-state error is too large, add a lag: a pole and zero very close to the origin, with the zero further left.

Lag compensator, root locus form (FPE 5.4.2)\[ D(s) = K\frac{s + z}{s + p}, \qquad z \gt p, \qquad \frac{z}{p} = \alpha \]
\(z,\,p\)
Zero and pole locations, both small compared with \(|s_0|\).
\(\alpha\)
Factor by which the low-frequency gain (and so \(K_v\)) rises.

Because the pair is close together and far from \(s_0\), their angles at \(s_0\) nearly cancel and the locus near the dominant poles hardly moves; yet at \(s = 0\) the compensator gain is \(z/p = \alpha\). The class rule: put the pole about two decades below the natural frequency of the desired pole, then choose \(z = \alpha p\) to meet the error requirement.

Worked example 5.1 Adding a lag for ramp error

\(G(s) = \dfrac{1}{s(s+4)}\) in unity feedback. The closed-loop poles must have \(\zeta = 0.5\) and the unit-ramp error must not exceed 0.05.

1
\(\zeta = 0.5\) on the locus of \(G\): the branches run along \(\mathrm{Re}(s) = -2\), and \(\theta = \sin^{-1}0.5 = 30^\circ\) gives \(s_0 = -2 \pm 2\sqrt3 j\) (\(\omega_n = 4\)). The phase condition holds (it is on the locus).
2
Magnitude condition: \(K = |s_0|\,|s_0 + 4| = 4 \times 4 = 16\).
3
Error with P control alone (FVT): \(K_v = \lim_{s\to0} s\,\dfrac{16}{s(s+4)} = 4\), so \(e_{ss} = 0.25\). Too large.
4
Need \(K_v = 20\): \(\alpha = 20/4 = 5\).
5
Pole two decades below \(\omega_n\): \(p = 4/100 = 0.04\). Zero \(z = \alpha p = 0.2\).
\(D(s) = 16\,\dfrac{s + 0.2}{s + 0.04}\). The closed-loop poles move only slightly, to \(-1.92 \pm 3.42j\), with a slow pole at \(-0.208\) that is nearly cancelled by the zero at −0.2. \(K_v = 16 \times 5/4 = 20\), so the ramp error is 0.05.

Figure 5.1: Near the dominant poles the locus is almost the original vertical line. The small loop near the origin is the lag pole-zero pair; the closed-loop pole trapped there is slow but has a small residue, which shows up as a long, low tail in the step response.

06Practice problems

Problem 1. Is \(s_0 = -0.75 + 1.39j\) on the locus of \(L = \dfrac{s^2+1}{s(s^2+4)}\)? If so, find \(K\). (Tutorial 4 problem 1.)

\(\angle L(s_0) = -180.0^\circ\), so yes. \(K = 1/|L(s_0)| = 2.50\) (computed).

Problem 2. Sketch the locus of \(L = \dfrac{1}{s(s+3)(s+6)}\): centroid, asymptote angles, break-away point and imaginary-axis crossing.

Centroid \(-3\); asymptotes ±60°, 180°. Break-away from \(3s^2 + 18s + 18 = 0\): \(s = -1.27\). Substituting \(s = j\omega\) into \(s^3 + 9s^2 + 18s + K = 0\): \(\omega^2 = 18\) and \(K = 9\omega^2 = 162\), \(s = \pm j\sqrt{18} = \pm 4.24j\).

Problem 3. Write \(s^3 + 5s^2 + (6 + c)s + 2c = 0\) in root locus form with \(c\) as the parameter.

\(s^3 + 5s^2 + 6s + c(s + 2) = 0 \Rightarrow 1 + c\,\dfrac{s+2}{s(s+2)(s+3)} = 0\), which simplifies to \(1 + c/(s(s+3))\) (note the cancellation, which hides a fixed root at −2).

Problem 4. In example 5.1, why not place the lag pole at 0.4 instead of 0.04?

With \(p = 0.4\), \(z = 2\): the pair is no longer small compared with \(|s_0| = 4\), its net angle at \(s_0\) is several degrees of lag, and the dominant poles move noticeably towards lower damping.

07Formula sheet

Root locus form

\(1 + KL(s) = 0\), \(L = b/a\)

Conditions

\(\sum\psi_i - \sum\phi_i = 180^\circ + 360^\circ(l-1)\)

\(K = \prod|s_0 - p_i|/\prod|s_0 - z_i|\)

Sketching

Real axis: left of odd count

\(\alpha = (\sum p_i - \sum z_i)/(n-m)\)

\(\phi_l = (180^\circ + 360^\circ(l-1))/(n-m)\)

Break-away: \(\frac{d}{ds}(a/b) = 0\)

Compensators

Lead: \(K(s+z)/(s+p)\), \(p \gt z\); zero near \(\omega_n\)

Lag: \(z/p = \alpha\), pole ≈ \(\omega_n/100\)
