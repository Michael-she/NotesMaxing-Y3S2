Q1Specifications and PID control[26]

The plant

\[ G(s) = \frac{4}{(s+1)(s+4)} \]

must give a unit-step response that meets all three requirements:

  1. overshoot \(M_p \le 10\) %;
  2. 2 % settling time \(t_s \le 2.5\) s;
  3. zero steady-state error, \(y_{ss} = 1\).
(a)

The plant is driven open loop, with no controller. Find its poles and use the Final Value Theorem to find \(y_{ss}\) for a unit step. State, with reasons, which of the three requirements are met.

[6]
(b)

The plant is placed in a unity negative feedback loop with proportional control \(D(s) = k_P\).

(i)

Find the closed-loop transfer function, and express \(\omega_n\) and \(\zeta\) in terms of \(k_P\).

(4)
(ii)

Find the largest \(k_P\) that meets requirement 1.

(4)
(iii)

With that gain, find \(t_s\) and the steady-state error to a unit step. Which requirements are met?

(4)
[12]
(c)

The controller is changed to the PI controller \(D(s) = k_P\,\dfrac{s+1}{s}\), so that its zero cancels the plant pole at \(-1\).

(i)

Using system type, explain why this controller removes the steady-state error to a step.

(3)
(ii)

Find the range of \(k_P\) that meets requirement 1, choose \(k_P = 2.5\), and estimate \(t_r\), \(t_p\), \(M_p\) and \(t_s\). Do all three requirements hold?

(5)
[8]
Memo for question 1

Approach. Check the open-loop plant against each requirement first. With P control, express \(\omega_n\) and \(\zeta\) in \(k_P\) and turn the overshoot limit into a bound on \(\zeta\). The PI zero cancels a pole, which keeps the loop second order.

(a)Open-loop plant[6]

  1. Poles: \((s+1)(s+4) = 0\), so \(s = -1\) and \(s = -4\).

    1

  2. Both are real, so the response has no oscillation and no overshoot: requirement 1 holds.

    1

  3. The FVT applies: \(sY(s) = G(s)\) has its poles in the left half plane.

    1

  4. \(y_{ss} = \lim_{s\to0} sG(s)\tfrac1s = G(0) = 4/4 = 1\): requirement 3 holds.

    1

  5. The slow pole sets the settling time: \(\sigma = 1\) gives \(t_s \approx 4/1 = 4\) s.

    1

  6. \(4 \gt 2.5\) s: requirement 2 fails. (Exactly, \(y = 1 - \tfrac43e^{-t} + \tfrac13e^{-4t}\), which settles at 4.2 s.)

    1
Requirements 1 and 3 hold; requirement 2 fails.

(b)Proportional control[12]

  1. (i) \(DG = \dfrac{4k_P}{(s+1)(s+4)}\) and \(T = \dfrac{DG}{1 + DG}\).
    1
  2. \[ T(s) = \frac{4k_P}{s^2 + 5s + 4 + 4k_P} \]
    1

  3. Matching \(s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_n s + \omega_n^2\): \(\omega_n = \sqrt{4 + 4k_P}\).

    1

  4. \(2\zeta\omega_n = 5\), so \(\zeta = \dfrac{5}{2\sqrt{4 + 4k_P}}\).

    1
  5. (ii) Rearrange the overshoot formula derive: \(\zeta = \dfrac{-\ln M_p}{\sqrt{\pi^2 + \ln^2 M_p}} = \dfrac{2.303}{\sqrt{9.870 + 5.302}} = 0.591\) for \(M_p = 0.1\).
    1

  6. \(\zeta \ge 0.591\) needs \(\sqrt{4 + 4k_P} \le \dfrac{5}{2 \times 0.591} = 4.23\).

    1

  7. \(4 + 4k_P \le 17.9\).

    1

  8. \(k_P \le 3.47\).

    1
  9. (iii) \(\sigma = \zeta\omega_n = 5/2 = 2.5\) for every \(k_P\).
    1

  10. \(t_s = 4/2.5 = 1.6\) s: requirement 2 holds.

    1

  11. Type 0: \(K_p = k_P\,G(0) = 3.47\), so \(e_{ss} = \dfrac{1}{1 + K_p} = 0.224\) derive.

    1

  12. Requirement 3 fails. Raising \(k_P\) to cut the error breaks requirement 1, so P control cannot meet all three.

    1
\(k_P = 3.47\): requirements 1 and 2 hold, \(e_{ss} = 0.224\).

(c)PI control[8]

  1. (i) The integrator puts a pole at \(s = 0\) in \(DG\): the loop is Type 1.
    1

  2. \(K_p = \lim_{s\to0} DG = \infty\).

    1

  3. \(e_{ss} = \lim_{s\to0} s\,\dfrac{1}{1 + DG}\,\dfrac1s = 0\) for a step.

    1
  4. (ii) \(DG = \dfrac{4k_P}{s(s+4)}\), so \(T = \dfrac{4k_P}{s^2 + 4s + 4k_P}\): \(\omega_n = 2\sqrt{k_P}\) and \(\zeta = \dfrac{4}{2\omega_n} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k_P}}\).
    1

  5. \(\zeta \ge 0.591\) needs \(k_P \le 1/0.591^2 = 2.86\) (the poles are complex for \(k_P \gt 1\); below that there is no overshoot at all).

    1

  6. With \(k_P = 2.5\): \(\omega_n = 3.16\) rad/s, \(\zeta = 0.632\), \(\sigma = 2\), \(\omega_d = \sqrt{10 - 4} = 2.45\) rad/s.

    1

  7. \(t_r \approx 1.8/3.16 = 0.57\) s; \(t_p = \pi/2.45 = 1.28\) s.

    1

  8. \(M_p = e^{-\pi(0.632)/0.775} = 7.7\) % and \(t_s = 4/2 = 2.0\) s: all three requirements hold.

    1
\(D(s) = 2.5\,\dfrac{s+1}{s}\) (\(k_P = k_I = 2.5\)): \(t_r = 0.57\) s, \(t_p = 1.28\) s, \(M_p = 7.7\) %, \(t_s = 2.0\) s.

Check. Simulation: 0.61 s, 1.28 s, 7.7 %, 1.89 s. The cancelled pole at −1 is still in the loop: invisible from the reference, it sets a slow 4 s decay for a disturbance at the plant input.

Q2Frequency-response lead design[26]

The plant

\[ G(s) = \frac{10}{s(s+2)} \]

is placed in unity feedback with the lead compensator

\[ D(s) = K\,\frac{Ts+1}{\alpha Ts+1}, \qquad 0 \lt \alpha \lt 1. \]

The closed loop must have a steady-state error of at most 0.05 to a unit ramp, and a phase margin of at least 45°.

(a)

Find the gain \(K\) that meets the ramp-error requirement.

[4]
(b)

With this \(K\) and no lead network, find the gain crossover frequency and the phase margin. Is the requirement met?

[6]
(c)

Design the lead network, allowing 10° extra for the shift in crossover. Give \(\alpha\), \(T\), the new crossover frequency and \(D(s)\) with all values substituted.

[12]
(d)

Check the phase margin of the compensated loop, and use the formula sheet's \(M_p\) against phase margin figure to estimate the overshoot. A simulation shows 22 % overshoot. Explain the difference.

[4]
Memo for question 2

Approach. Fix \(K\) from the ramp error, measure the margin of \(KG\), then design the lead so its maximum phase sits at the new crossover.

(a)Gain from the ramp error[4]

  1. Type 1 loop: \(K_v = \lim_{s\to0} sDG\) derive, and the lead factor is 1 at \(s = 0\).

    1

  2. \(K_v = K \times 10/2 = 5K\).

    1

  3. \(e_{ss} = 1/K_v \le 0.05\) needs \(K_v \ge 20\).

    1

  4. Solve.

    1
\(K = 4\)

(b)Margin without the lead[6]

  1. \(|KG(j\omega)| = \dfrac{40}{\omega\sqrt{\omega^2 + 4}} = 1\).

    1

  2. \(\omega^4 + 4\omega^2 - 1600 = 0\).

    1

  3. \(\omega^2 = \dfrac{-4 + \sqrt{16 + 6400}}{2} = 38.05\), so \(\omega_c = 6.17\) rad/s.

    1

  4. \(\angle KG = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(6.17/2) = -90^\circ - 72.0^\circ = -162.0^\circ\).

    1

  5. PM \(= 18.0^\circ\).

    1

  6. \(18^\circ \lt 45^\circ\): the requirement fails.

    1

(c)Lead design[12]

  1. Phase needed: \(\phi_{max} = 45^\circ - 18^\circ + 10^\circ = 37^\circ\).

    1

  2. From \(\sin\phi_{max} = \dfrac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\) derive (or read \(1/\alpha \approx 4\) off the formula-sheet figure): \(\alpha = \dfrac{1 - \sin37^\circ}{1 + \sin37^\circ} = \dfrac{1 - 0.602}{1 + 0.602} = 0.249\).

    2

  3. The lead adds gain \(1/\sqrt\alpha\) at \(\omega_{max}\), so put \(\omega_{max}\) where \(|KG| = \sqrt\alpha = 0.499\) (−6.0 dB).

    1

  4. \(\omega\sqrt{\omega^2 + 4} = 40/0.499 = 80.2\): \(\omega^4 + 4\omega^2 - 6432 = 0\), \(\omega^2 = 78.25\), so \(\omega_c = 8.85\) rad/s.

    2

  5. \(\omega_{max} = 1/(T\sqrt\alpha)\) (formula sheet): \(T = \dfrac{1}{8.85 \times 0.499} = 0.227\) s.

    2

  6. \(\alpha T = 0.249 \times 0.227 = 0.0564\) s.

    1

  7. Zero at \(1/T = 4.41\) rad/s, pole at \(1/(\alpha T) = 17.7\) rad/s.

    1

  8. Substitute.

    2
\(D(s) = 4\,\dfrac{0.227s + 1}{0.0564s + 1}\)

(d)Check and overshoot[4]

  1. At 8.85 rad/s: \(\angle KG = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}4.42 = -167.3^\circ\); the lead adds 37.0°, so PM ≈ 49.7°: the requirement holds.

    1

  2. The formula-sheet figure gives \(M_p \approx 0.2\) at a 50° margin.

    1

  3. The figure assumes a second-order loop with no zeros.

    1

  4. The lead's zero at −4.41 rad/s also appears in the closed loop, close to the dominant poles (\(-6.68 \pm 8.15j\) and −6.39), and a nearby zero adds overshoot (week 1): hence 22 %.

    1

Q3Root-locus design[28]

The plant

\[ G(s) = \frac{1}{s(s+1)(s+5)} \]

is placed in a unity negative feedback loop. Use the root-locus method throughout.

3.1

With proportional control \(D = K\), sketch the root locus for \(K \gt 0\). Give the real-axis segments, the centroid and angles of the asymptotes, the break-away point, and the gain and frequency at which the locus crosses the imaginary axis.

[10]
3.2

Find the gain \(K\) that gives dominant closed-loop poles with \(\zeta = 0.5\). Find the third closed-loop pole, and estimate the settling time and the velocity error constant \(K_v\).

[8]
3.3

The dominant poles must instead sit at \(s_0 = -2 \pm j2\sqrt3\) (\(\zeta = 0.5\), \(\omega_n = 4\) rad/s). Design the lead compensator \(D(s) = K\,\dfrac{s+1}{s+p}\), whose zero cancels the plant pole at −1. Use the angle condition to find \(p\) and the magnitude condition to find \(K\). Give \(K_v\) and comment on the cancellation.

[10]
Memo for question 3

Approach. Apply the sketching rules in order. For 3.2, search the \(\zeta = 0.5\) line for the angle condition. For 3.3, the cancellation leaves a simple angle problem for the pole.

3.1Root locus with proportional control[10]

  1. \(L = \dfrac{1}{s(s+1)(s+5)}\): three poles, no zeros, so three branches from 0, −1 and −5, all going to infinity.

    1

  2. Real axis: \([-1, 0]\) and \((-\infty, -5]\), left of an odd number of poles.

    1

  3. Centroid \(\alpha = (0 - 1 - 5)/3 = -2\).

    1

  4. Asymptote angles \((180^\circ + 360^\circ l)/3 = 60^\circ, 180^\circ, 300^\circ\).

    1

  5. Break-away from \(dK/ds = 0\) with \(K = -(s^3 + 6s^2 + 5s)\): \(3s^2 + 12s + 5 = 0\).

    1

  6. \(s = \dfrac{-12 \pm \sqrt{144 - 60}}{6} = -0.473\) or \(-3.53\); only −0.473 lies on the locus.

    1

  7. Substitute \(s = j\omega\) into \(s^3 + 6s^2 + 5s + K = 0\). Imaginary part: \(-\omega^3 + 5\omega = 0\), so \(\omega = \sqrt5 = 2.24\) rad/s.

    1

  8. Real part: \(-6\omega^2 + K = 0\), so \(K = 30\).

    1

  9. Sketch with these features labelled.

    2

3.2Gain for \(\zeta = 0.5\)[8]

  1. \(\zeta = 0.5\) is the line at 60° from the negative real axis.

    1

  2. Search along it for \(\angle L = -180^\circ\): \(s = -0.417 \pm 0.722j\) (\(\omega_n = 0.833\) rad/s).

    2

  3. Magnitude condition: \(K = |s||s+1||s+5| = 0.834 \times 0.928 \times 4.64 = 3.59\).

    1

  4. The closed-loop poles sum to −6 (the \(s^2\) coefficient), so the third pole is at \(-6 + 2(0.417) = -5.17\).

    1

  5. That is twelve times further out than \(\sigma = 0.417\): the pair dominates.

    1

  6. \(t_s \approx 4/0.417 = 9.6\) s.

    1

  7. \(K_v = \lim_{s\to0} sKL = K/5 = 0.72\).

    1
\(K = 3.59\), third pole −5.17, \(t_s \approx 9.6\) s, \(K_v = 0.72\).

3.3Lead by root locus[10]

  1. With the zero cancelling the pole at −1, the loop is \(\dfrac{K}{s(s+5)(s+p)}\).

    1

  2. Angles at \(s_0 = -2 + 3.464j\): from 0, 120.0°; from −5, \(\tan^{-1}(3.464/3) = 49.1^\circ\).

    2

  3. Angle condition: \(120.0^\circ + 49.1^\circ + \phi_p = 180^\circ\), so \(\phi_p = 10.9^\circ\).

    2

  4. \(p = 2 + 3.464/\tan10.9^\circ = 20.0\).

    1

  5. Magnitude condition: \(K = |s_0||s_0+5||s_0+20| = 4 \times 4.583 \times 18.33 = 336\).

    2

  6. \(K_v = 336/(5 \times 20) = 3.36\): almost five times the proportional design, with \(t_s \approx 4/2 = 2\) s.

    1

  7. The other closed-loop poles are −21.0 and −1. The pole at −1 is cancelled in \(Y/R\) so it does not show in the step response, but it still appears in the response to a disturbance at the plant input, and an imperfect cancellation leaves a slow, small tail.

    1
\(D(s) = 336\,\dfrac{s+1}{s+20}\)
